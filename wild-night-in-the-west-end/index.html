FICTION FILE 01 · WORK IN PROGRESS

Wild Night
In The West End

A noisy, tender ensemble novel about people trying to be understood while performing versions of themselves — in bedrooms, bars, backstage corridors, and a city approaching a breaking point.

CONTEMPORARY ENSEMBLE FICTIONTHEATRE / INTIMACY / POLITICAL UNRESTMANUSCRIPT IN DEVELOPMENT
01 / THE STORYPRIVATE LIVES · PUBLIC CONSEQUENCES

A city full of noise.
People trying to connect.

Wild Night In The West End moves between chaotic humour, romantic vulnerability, theatre, friendship, and political anger. Its characters rarely fit neatly into the versions of themselves they present to other people. A ridiculous night can expose real cruelty; an ordinary breakfast can feel more consequential than a declaration; a family secret can turn private grief into public history.

The novel is built as an ensemble: separate lives and tonal registers gradually begin to touch. The aim isn't to make every character part of every plot, but to let their different experiences alter the meaning of the larger story.

Some nights change nothing. Some nights quietly change the shape of everything.

What's taking shape.

The manuscript is still growing. The next stretch develops the theatre ensemble around Leslie, Phoebe, and Ludwig, while widening the view of Dylan and the people inside the revolutionary movement. Their stories will add perspectives rather than flatten the novel into a single explanation of events.

The backstage worldLeslie, Phoebe, Ludwig, Jane, and Rhian — performance, labour, ambition, and the lives around the stage.
The movementDylan and other revolutionaries, with different motives and ideas of what a better future should mean.
The convergenceSeparate character stories gradually meet; the final gathering is intended to let people tell their own versions without erasing ambiguity.
